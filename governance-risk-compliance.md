# Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)

Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) is about how organizations manage security in a structured and consistent way. It connects policies, risk decisions, and compliance requirements with how systems actually operate.

In practice, GRC is where many security decisions happen. It helps answer questions such as what needs to be fixed, what risks are acceptable, and what the organization is accountable for.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of GRC is to create structure around security.

It helps organizations:

* Understand and prioritize risk
* Align security with business needs
* Meet regulatory and internal requirements
* Maintain consistency across systems and processes

Without this structure, security becomes reactive and harder to manage over time.

---

## Governance

Governance defines how security is managed across the organization.

This includes:

* Policies and standards
* Roles and responsibilities
* Decision-making processes
* Accountability

Strong governance helps ensure security is applied consistently rather than handled differently across teams.

---

## Risk Management

Risk management focuses on identifying and handling potential threats.

This typically involves:

* Identifying risks
* Evaluating impact and likelihood
* Prioritizing what matters most
* Deciding how to respond

Not every risk is treated the same. Some risks are fixed, some are accepted, and others are reduced over time.

A lot of real-world security work comes down to deciding where to focus effort and resources.

---

## Compliance

Compliance is about meeting required standards and expectations.

This can include:

* Internal policies
* Industry standards such as NIST or ISO
* Regulatory requirements
* Audit and reporting activities

Compliance often involves documentation and validation, but it is important to recognize that compliance does not always mean security.

---

## Key Activities

### Risk Assessments

Risk assessments help determine what issues matter most.

They involve:

* Identifying assets and risks
* Evaluating impact and likelihood
* Assigning risk levels
* Recommending actions

Good assessments help teams focus on what actually needs attention.

### Security Controls

Security controls are safeguards used to reduce risk.

Examples include:

* Access controls
* Logging and monitoring
* Encryption
* System configuration controls

The key is not just having controls in place, but knowing they are working as intended.

### Third-Party Risk

Third-party risk comes from vendors and external systems.

This includes:

* Reviewing vendor security
* Assessing risk before integration
* Monitoring ongoing access and exposure

In many environments, external systems introduce significant risk that organizations cannot ignore.

---

## Practical Considerations

GRC is not about eliminating all risk. That is not realistic.

It is about:

* Making informed decisions
* Balancing security with business needs
* Understanding trade-offs

Often the challenge is not identifying risk. The challenge is deciding what to do about it and getting agreement across teams.

---

## Summary

GRC provides the structure behind how security is managed.

It helps ensure that risks are understood, controls are meaningful, and decisions are made intentionally.

When done well, GRC supports the rest of a security program and improves consistency across the organization.
